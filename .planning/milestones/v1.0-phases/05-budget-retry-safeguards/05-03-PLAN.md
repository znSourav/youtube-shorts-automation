<objective>
Move the two language-model dispatch points onto the real budget, and close the check-then-record race for them by making the check, the paid call, and the spend record one serialized unit.

Purpose: 01-REVIEW-FIX.md's WR-02 flagged that two callers can both pass the pre-flight check against the same pre-dispatch snapshot before either records anything. Phase 4 patched that for video only. Now that the gate guards real money rather than a development convenience, the fix has to cover every dispatch type — and the record has to land inside the serialized window, not after it.
Output: a shared dispatch mutex, a record-at-the-money-boundary write path, and two re-pointed dispatch sites.
</objective>

<design_note>
**Why the spend record moves to the dispatch boundary.** Today `runStoryDirector` records to the development ledger immediately, while its durable `GenerationRecord` counterpart is accumulated in an array and flushed by `create-story.ts` only after the story row is saved. That deferral is harmless while the durable record is a secondary copy. It stops being harmless the moment that table becomes the enforcement source of truth, for two separate reasons:

1. **A lost dispatch.** `create-story.ts` returns early on a blocked response, a parse failure, a validation failure, and a persistence failure — on none of those paths is the accumulated spend flushed. Those calls were dispatched and billed. Under the new scheme they would simply never count against her budget.
2. **A reopened race.** Serializing only the check and the call leaves the record outside the serialized window, so a second caller can still read a stale total during the entire remaining story workflow — seconds, not microseconds.

The fix is to write each record where the money is actually spent, with a null `storyId` (made possible by plan 05-02's schema change), and to link those rows to the story afterwards once its id exists. This keeps the story association Phase 3 built, records every dispatched call unconditionally, and puts the write inside the mutex where it belongs.
</design_note>
